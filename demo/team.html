I

The underwriting pipeline runs in a single session — not three to five days.

The whole quote-to-bind cycle is a four-stage automated pipeline: data aggregation in under three seconds, AI underwriting in under thirty, a bindable quote in under two minutes, and a same-day bind — against a 3–5 day industry standard (Self-Insure operating model, May 2026). Speed is not a feature here; it is the cost structure and the conversion rate.

Why the speed compounds

A 3–5 day cycle is an artifact of hand-offs — between aggregators, underwriters, and service desks — that an AI-native build simply does not have. Self-Insure's pipeline keeps the case inside one autonomous workflow from intake to issue. The same architecture that delivers a same-day bind is the one that runs at an 8% expense ratio: there is no separate "fast lane," only the absence of legacy headcount.

II

Autonomous AI agents do the work incumbents staff with people — 60–70% of it.

Across underwriting, claims, and service, autonomous agents are designed to replace 60–70% of the headcount a traditional insurer carries — a Self-Insure operating-model assumption (May 2026), to be validated against a staffed org plan, not yet a measured result. The human team is deliberately small and senior — actuarial judgment, regulatory ownership, exception handling, and oversight — not a call-centre. That design is the structural source of the targeted ~52.7% operating-cost advantage.

III

84% combined ratio at scale — 55 loss + 8 LAE + 13 commission + 8 opex.

The unit economics are decomposed, not asserted. At scale the combined ratio is 84% — fifteen points of underwriting margin against an industry that runs near 99% (Self-Insure 10-Year Proforma v03, May 2026). The 8-point operating-expense line, against a 25–30% industry norm, is the AI-native edge made explicit. The 13-point commission is deliberate, not inherited: even a direct-first carrier funds agent and partner channels to acquire policies — the AI advantage shows up in the 8-point opex line, not by zeroing out distribution.

What the 4.2% number actually says

Across 10,000 simulated paths — varying loss frequency, severity, catastrophe timing, and rate environment — only 4.2% end in regulatory insolvency (RBC falling below 200%). Read alongside the 91% probability of a positive Year-10 outcome and the bounded downside, the engine is not arguing that nothing goes wrong; it is quantifying how rarely the tail is fatal. The distribution is the product — not the 6.2× upside on its own.

VI

The ROP reserve plan: cede first for speed, bring it in-house for margin.

A 30-year return-of-premium obligation is a long-duration liability with no established NAIC reserving precedent — which is exactly what blocks naive entrants and exactly the regulatory problem this team is built to solve. The plan is a two-track path: a 100% quota-share cession to a life reinsurer for fast approval, transitioning to an in-house CRVM-analog reserve by Year 3 (Self-Insure proforma v03).

The two-track regulatory path
Years 1–3 · Option 3

100% cession to a life reinsurer

A 100% quota-share cession of the ROP obligation to a life reinsurer secures fast approval — roughly 5–9 months to a licensed launch — and removes the reserving question from the critical path while the book is young.

By Year 3 · Option 1

CRVM-analog reserve, in-house

Transitioning to an in-house CRVM-analog reserve once scaled captures the margin that ceding gives away — saving roughly $47M versus staying ceded across the plan.

Throughout

Trust over-collateralized by design

The supporting trust is sized to stay ahead of the ROP liability at every point — the promise to return premium is collateralized, not aspirational.

Two-track plan, Self-Insure proforma v03. Reserving methodology to be certified by a credentialed actuary; verify with counsel.

$4.0M of the $15M seed builds the founding team: a Chief Actuary, CFO, CTO, and COO, plus core staff (Self-Insure use-of-funds). These are the credentialed owners the AI stack reports up to — the people who hold the actuarial certification, the capital discipline, the engineering of the underwriting engine, and the regulatory launch. The four founding hires are not managers of large departments — they are the credentialed owners of the four things that cannot be automated: actuarial judgment, capital discipline, the engine itself, and the regulatory launch. The $4.0M team line in the seed funds exactly these seats plus core staff.
